Implicit Association Test (IAT)

This classroom demonstration follows the five-stage structure of Experiment 3 in Greenwald, McGhee, and Schwartz (1998), using female names and pleasant/unpleasant word categories.

Categories used in this test

Social categories: Black and White

The test uses first names that were selected in the original study because they were considered more commonly associated with Black or White people in the United States at that time. In the task, these names serve as cues for the social categories Black and White. A name is not a reliable way to determine any real person's racial identity.

Attribute categories: Pleasant and Unpleasant

You will also classify words with positive or pleasant meanings (such as love, peace, and happy) and words with negative or unpleasant meanings (such as grief, ugly, and disaster).

What the test examines

Across the combined stages, you will sometimes classify Black-associated names together with pleasant words and White-associated names together with unpleasant words, and sometimes the pairings will be reversed. The task compares how quickly and accurately you respond to these pairings. Differences in response speed may reflect the relative strength of learned associations, but they do not by themselves establish what a person consciously believes or how they behave.

These categories do not imply that either racial group is inherently more pleasant or unpleasant. The pleasant and unpleasant labels describe the words, not the people.

Important educational note

This shortened demonstration is intended for learning about reaction-time tasks and implicit associations. It is not a diagnostic tool, and individual scores should not be interpreted as definitive measures of prejudice, character, or behavior.

How to respond

Use the A key for the category shown on the left and the L key for the category shown on the right. Categorize each item as quickly and accurately as possible. If you make an error, an error message appears briefly; continue with the next item.

There are five stages and 90 trials in total: 10 trials in each single-category stage, and 10 practice + 20 scored trials in each combined stage. Press Space to advance through instruction and transition screens.

Accuracy

What does this score mean?

  • The IAT compares response speed across two pairings of categories. A faster pairing can be consistent with stronger relative associations.
  • The result is relative: it does not measure an absolute attitude toward either group.
  • Reaction times are affected by many factors, including familiarity with the names, attention, task order, key mapping, and general speed.
  • This shortened, fixed-order classroom version is not suitable for research or individual diagnosis. The original study counterbalanced combined-task order and used more trials.
  • The female-name categories were selected for a U.S. context in the late 1990s. Their associations may not be familiar or meaningful to students in other cultural contexts.
Discussion questions
  1. Did the two combined tasks feel equally easy?
  2. What factors besides associations could explain a reaction-time difference?
  3. How should we interpret implicit measures when they differ from explicit self-reports?
  4. What would need to change before this task could be used in a study with Korean participants?
